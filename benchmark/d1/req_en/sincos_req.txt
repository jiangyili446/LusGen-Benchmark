The node integrator receives three real inputs F, STEP, and init, and returns a real output Y. It implements a discrete numerical integrator. At the initial instant, Y is init. From the second cycle onward, Y adds to the previous Y an integration increment computed by the trapezoidal rule: the average of current F and previous F, multiplied by STEP.

The node sincos receives a real input omega and returns two real outputs sin and cos, intended to generate sine and cosine signals related to omega. It defines two intermediate state variables pcos and psin. Initially, pcos is 1.0 and psin is 0.0; afterward, pcos takes the previous value of cos and psin takes the previous value of sin. The node calls integrator twice: sin is omega multiplied by the integral of pcos with step 0.1 and initial value 0.0; cos is omega multiplied by the integral of -psin with step 0.1 and initial value 1.0. The overall behavior uses previous cos and sin values as feedback for the derivative relation and recursively generates sin and cos in discrete time. The Chinese requirement states that initially sin starts from 0.0 and cos starts from 1.0.
